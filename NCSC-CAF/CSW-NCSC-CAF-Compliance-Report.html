Cisco Secure Workload — UK NCSC Cyber Assessment Framework (CAF)

Compliance Report | Operators of Essential Services & GovAssure

Framework: NCSC Cyber Assessment Framework (CAF v3.2, November 2024) Regulatory context: NIS Regulations 2018 (UK) for OES and RDSPs; GovAssure for UK central government departments and arm's-length bodies Audience: CISO, CAF lead / GRC, Competent Authority-facing assurance teams, GovAssure sponsors

How to read this report

CAF is a principle-based, outcome-focused assessment framework. It does not prescribe technology; it asks whether an operator can demonstrate the outcome for the systems that support each essential function, at one of three IGP (Indicator of Good Practice) maturity levels:

IGP status Meaning to the Competent Authority
Achieved Outcome is fully met with formalised, automated, continuously verified controls.
Partially Achieved Basic controls exist or are implemented across parts of the estate, but gaps or manual inconsistencies remain.
Not Achieved Controls are inadequate; unacceptable risk to essential function.

This report describes the CAF principles Cisco Secure Workload (CSW) directly addresses, the principles it partially addresses, and the principles that must be evidenced by other controls. It is not an NCSC certification; it is a mapping to help operators structure their evidence submission and identify credible "Partially Achieved → Achieved" moves.

1. Executive Summary

Cisco Secure Workload gives UK CNI operators and central-government departments continuous, machine-generated evidence for a specific and important subset of the 14 CAF principles — the outcomes where regulators most consistently mark operators as Partially Achieved because the evidence is a static diagram, a quarterly spreadsheet, or an annual firewall sample.

The CAF outcomes where CSW is a primary evidence source are:

  • A3 Asset Management — continuous, real-time inventory of workloads and services supporting each essential function, across on-prem and cloud.
  • B5 Resilient Networks & Systems — structural segmentation between corporate IT, IT/OT DMZ, and OT/SCADA tiers; microsegmentation within each tier; behaviour-derived allowlists.
  • C1 Security Monitoring — continuous flow and process telemetry with synchronised time, forwarded to the SIEM/SOAR.
  • C2 Proactive Event Discovery — behavioural anomaly detection, reachability-weighted vulnerability prioritisation, and threat-intel-fed workspace rules.

The outcomes where CSW is a partial or supporting evidence source are: A1 Governance, A2 Risk Management, A4 Supply Chain, B1 Service Protection Policies & Processes, B2 Identity & Access Control, B3 Data Security, B4 System Security, D1 Response & Recovery Planning, and D2 Improvements.

The outcome not addressed by CSW is B6 Staff Awareness & Training (organisational / HR).

Deploying CSW as the "continuous-evidence tier" is a defensible way to move A3, B5, C1, C2, and the forensic pillar of D1 from Partially Achieved to Achieved in the next CAF review or GovAssure engagement.

4. Out-of-Scope and Complementary Controls

The following CAF outcomes are not addressed by CSW and must be evidenced by other controls, processes, or products:

CAF area Why CSW is not the evidence source Where to source evidence
A1 Board / SMT governance decisions Organisational — not a technical control Board minutes; security-committee terms of reference
A2 Enterprise risk methodology + treatment plans Process — CSW provides inputs, not the methodology GRC platform; risk register
A4 Supplier contracts, audits, and assurance clauses Procurement — CSW helps reconcile egress but does not author contracts Contract management system; supplier assurance reports
B1 Written policy authoring, exception register, management review Policy governance — CSW enforces, does not author ISMS documentation; management review minutes
B2 MFA, PAM, IAM, credential lifecycle Identity plane — CSW consumes identity context but does not authenticate users Okta / Entra ID / CyberArk / equivalent
B3 Cryptographic primitives, keys, HSM governance CSW enforces protected paths; does not manage keys KMS / HSM inventory; key ceremony evidence
B4 Patching operation itself (deploying the patch) CSW prioritises; does not deploy Patch management platform (SCCM, Intune, Landscape, Ansible, Salt)
B5 OT device-layer segmentation (Purdue L0–L2) CSW is IT-side; does not enforce on PLCs / RTUs / IEDs Cisco Cyber Vision / Claroty xDome / Nozomi Networks
B6 Training programme, phishing simulations Organisational — HR / learning LMS; phishing-simulation platform
D1 Backups, immutability, RTO/RPO testing CSW does not perform backup or restore Backup platform; DR exercise reports
D2 Formal lessons-learned documentation Process — CSW supplies inputs, not the process artefact Incident management platform; management review

5. Suggested Next Steps

  1. Scope confirmation. Align on the list of essential functions in scope and confirm the sector profile the Competent Authority applies.
  2. Deploy CSW to the highest-value tier first — IT/OT DMZ workloads, jump hosts, and any workload with reach into an essential-function OT zone.
  3. Baseline for two weeks using ADM; walk the resulting flow map through with each essential-function owner.
  4. Design and simulate policy per essential function; do not enforce until a clean Simulation window and change-board sign-off.
  5. Wire telemetry to the SIEM/SOAR with NTP-verified timestamps — this is the C1 evidence baseline.
  6. Produce the first quarterly CAF pack per essential function (see the runbook's Audit preparation & evidence export section) and use it to identify credible Partially Achieved → Achieved moves with your Competent Authority.
  7. Repeat quarterly, capturing policy diffs and ADM refreshes; these are the D2 improvement inputs.

6. Disclaimer

This report maps Cisco Secure Workload capabilities to the outcomes described in the UK NCSC Cyber Assessment Framework (CAF v3.2, November 2024) for the purpose of aiding operator evidence collection. It is not an NCSC-endorsed certification, is not a substitute for an independent CAF assessment or GovAssure engagement, and does not confer any IGP maturity status. Awarding of IGP status is the responsibility of the operator's Competent Authority (Ofgem, DWI, CAA, ORR, MCA, DfT, DHSC/NHS England, Ofcom, ICO) or GovAssure assessor. Cisco Secure Workload is one component of an evidence programme that also depends on organisational governance, procurement, HR, business continuity, and training controls outside CSW's technical scope. Confirm your sector profile and any Competent Authority-specific guidance before finalising evidence.
